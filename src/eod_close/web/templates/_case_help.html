{# How to work a case: shared by the briefing, where it is read before the clock starts, and the work page, where it stays available but collapsed. #}
  1. Find pairs. Look for a bank entry and a ledger entry that are the same payment: usually the same reference, and the same amount or close to it.
  2. Link them. Tick both and choose Link selected, or type the same label, such as 1, in both Match group boxes. One bank payout can cover several ledger sales: give them all the same group.
  3. Explain the rest. If an entry has no partner, choose why in its Exception list. If two entries are the same payment but the amounts have swapped digits (£45.10 against £54.10), link them and mark both as a likely transposition.
  4. Close the day when every entry is linked or explained. Check progress tells you how many are left.

Why amounts differ:

  • BRAMBLE keeps a fee of 1.4% + 20p, so the bank receives less than the ledger shows.
  • QUILLFEATHER keeps 1.9% and pays a whole day's sales as one bank entry.
  • OTTERLINE pays the full amount, but usually two working days later. Any payment can reach the bank up to three working days after it was booked.
  • Euro sales (the euro amount is in brackets) can differ by a few pence after conversion.
  • Refunds and chargebacks have their own reference in the ledger; the order they reverse is shown after for. BRAMBLE also charges £15 for each chargeback.
