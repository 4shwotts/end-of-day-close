How it works

Every evening a finance team proves that the money the bank received matches what the company recorded. This tool does the matching and leaves a person only the lines it cannot explain.

Three steps

  1. {{ icon("upload") }}

    1 Load both lists

    The bank statement (the money that actually came in or went out) and the ledger (the company's own record of what it sold and refunded).

  2. {{ icon("spark") }}

    2 The engine matches and explains

    It pairs what it can, allowing for fees, late settlement (money arriving a day or two after the sale) and rounding, and says why in plain English.

  3. {{ icon("search") }}

    3 You review only the exceptions

    Anything it cannot explain, such as a missing, duplicated or mistyped entry, is listed with a suggested next step.

Why it is fast: the race

A real bank file can have tens of thousands of lines, so the engine has to be fast as well as right. Race it against the obvious approach on days of different sizes.

Checking every pair

For each bank line, search the whole ledger for its partner. Twice as many lines means about four times the work. (Quadratic time, O(n²).)

The engine

First builds indexes of the ledger, like the index at the back of a book, so finding a partner is a quick lookup. Twice as many lines means only a little over twice the work. (Close to linear time.)

On tiny days checking every pair can win, because building the indexes has a fixed cost; watch the gap open as the days grow. Times are measured live on this server, so they vary.
